## Findings

### Important
1. **Wheel is dead on a short window (named risk 1: a real defect).** VisualRoutingView.xaml.cs, DisplaysScroll_PreviewMouseWheel (around line 356-362) sets `e.Handled = true` unconditionally. With ScrollableWidth == 0 and ScrollableHeight > 0 (the 1008x615 / 1024x720 case the vertical safety net exists for), the wheel computes target 0, scrolls nothing and swallows the event, so the safety net can only be used by dragging the bar or by touch pan. It also swallows the wheel when nothing scrolls at all (nothing for an outer scroller to receive, if any).
   Minimal fix: at the top of the handler
   `if (DisplaysScroll.ScrollableWidth <= 0.5) return;` (leave Handled false so the ScrollViewer does its normal vertical wheel scroll). Optionally, when ScrollableWidth > 0 and ScrollableHeight > 0, keep horizontal (Shift+wheel could do vertical) and document it. Add a test: 2 displays at 1008x615, wheel -120 raises VerticalOffset > 0 / e.Handled false.

### Minor
2. **Arrow top margin stale/zero fallback (named risk 2).** UpdateDisplayArrows returns early (leaving Margin.Top unchanged, initially 0) when the first card or its PreviewSurface has no height yet. With Margin.Top 0 the 48 px arrow (x 8..56) sits over the first card's header row and the top of the action row, i.e. over the Layout dropdown (64x56 at the card's left) and, on the right, over the info badge / mode badge region. Only a transient: ScrollChanged/SizeChanged fire after layout so the picture is normally measured by then, and cards have a plan-fixed height (Undo toast does not change card height, chrome rows are fixed), so resize and the toast are fine (resize triggers SizeChanged, vertical offset changes trigger ScrollChanged, and the Math.Max(0, ...) clamp handles a first card scrolled out of view vertically). Zero cards: arrows hidden, no issue. Window narrower than 400: card keeps 400 (MinWidth), ScrollableWidth > 0 even for one card, arrows show over that card; acceptable. Hardening: when the picture is not measurable, hide the arrows (or default the margin to a sane centre) instead of leaving Margin.Top at 0.
3. **Overlap with card content (named risk 3).** Arrows are vertically centred on the picture, so they do NOT overlap the Layout dropdown, rename pencil or info badge (those live in header/action rows above the picture; the picture is >= ~190 px tall even at 400 px cards). They do cover a 48 px strip of the picture's left/right edge on the first/last card (the strip is also a drop target for drag-copy; the empty-state text is centred and fits). On the right, when the vertical safety-net bar shows, the arrow (right margin 8) sits over 8 px of the 16 px gutter, covering part of the 8 px thumb at that height. Cosmetic; consider Margin right 24 only while the vertical bar is visible, or leave it.
4. **Keyboard.** Arrows come after the ScrollViewer in document order, so Tab reaches every card (all realized, including off-screen ones) before the arrows; nothing scrolls an off-screen focused card into view (Task 4 BringIntoView: noted only). Also, when a focused arrow disables itself at an end (pressing Enter on the right arrow at the last step) WPF drops keyboard focus; Task 4 may want to move focus to the opposite arrow or the card.
5. **Theme.xaml Style.ScrollViewer.VerticalOverlay (named risk 4).** Keyed, not implicit, only DisplaysScroll uses it, so no effect on other ScrollViewers. Horizontal bar still shows (Row 1 gutter, same as the implicit style; PART_HorizontalScrollBar/PART_VerticalScrollBar present). Differences from the implicit style: (a) no `Hidden` triggers for either direction (only Disabled), so a reuse with Hidden would show an empty reserved gutter; (b) the 16 px horizontal gutter is reserved whenever HorizontalScrollBarVisibility=Auto, even for <= 4 displays that never scroll, so the row loses 16 px of height permanently (same behaviour as the implicit style, but new for this view, which previously had Horizontal Disabled and therefore no gutter). That height matters on short windows where the safety net exists. Consider collapsing HorizontalGutter unless ComputedHorizontalScrollBarVisibility is Visible (Trigger on that property), or document the choice. Also a copy of the whole template: duplicates ~45 lines; acceptable.
6. **Test quality (named risk 5).** Mostly real behaviour with real values (arrow step equals card width + 16 to 1 px, ends clamp, last card whole in viewport, centring within 2 px, 48 px size, automation names, 4 displays no arrows). Min-400 tests: Carousel_At992_Is400 (320 without change) and Carousel_At1232_UsesCarouselMin400 (338 without change) would fail without the change; Carousel_At1888_StaysW35 is a regression guard that passes either way (fine, intended). Weak spots: (a) `Assert.False(left.IsTabStop && left.IsEnabled)` is vacuous (IsTabStop is true from the style, IsEnabled false, so it passes for any state); assert on `!left.IsEnabled` only, or check `KeyboardNavigation`/focusability honestly. (b) Wheel test only checks direction and Handled, not magnitude or the ScrollableWidth == 0 case (finding 1 is untested). (c) No test for a window narrower than 400 or for resize while scrolled (Review Focus item; probably Task 5). (d) Touch pan only asserted via the PanningMode property. (e) The new tests assert the old plan wording "HorizontalScrollOnly" as vertical Auto per ruling, fine.
7. **Stale comments.** ViewSmokeTests.UiV2FullHd.cs keeps the old "Recorded at 1920x952 before the cap change... re-recorded at 640x360 ..." history directly under the new Round 3 comment, and the new comment says earlier baselines "(731 / 587 / 336 wide)" while the history lines mention other numbers; the combined block is confusing. Trim to the current rule.
